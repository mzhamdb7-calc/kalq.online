How calculations work

Most categories use a fixed factor through a shared base unit. Temperature uses scale and offset formulas, while fuel economy uses reciprocal formulas.

Regional clarity

  • US and imperial gallons are labelled separately.
  • US and imperial fluid ounces are not treated as equal.
  • Binary data units such as MiB remain distinct from decimal MB.
  • Country selection changes recommendations, not the underlying definition.

Practical tips

  • Use additional decimal places for engineering or scientific work.
  • Keep the unrounded result for chained calculations.
  • Check whether a recipe uses US, metric, Australian or imperial measures.
  • Use the unrounded result when a converted value will be used in another calculation.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing mass with weight or volume.
  • Using a multiplication factor for temperature or fuel economy.
  • Treating MB and MiB as identical.
  • Ignoring the difference between US and imperial gallons.
Are unit conversions exact?

Many conversions are exact because the units have fixed definitions. Displayed results can still be rounded, while some contextual or lookup-based conversions are approximate.

Why are US and imperial units separate?

Several units share a name but not a size. A US gallon, imperial gallon, US fluid ounce and imperial fluid ounce must therefore be labelled separately.

Why does temperature use a different formula?

Temperature scales have different interval sizes and zero points. Conversion needs both multiplication and an offset rather than a single factor.

What is the difference between MB and MiB?

One MB is 1,000,000 bytes, while one MiB is 1,048,576 bytes. The converter keeps decimal and binary data units distinct.

How many decimal places should I use?

Two to four decimal places are usually enough for everyday use. Scientific and engineering calculations may require more significant figures.

Does changing the country alter the value?

No. It changes suggested units and regional notes. Mathematical definitions remain unchanged unless you explicitly choose a regional unit such as an imperial gallon.
