GPA formula

A credit-weighted GPA gives courses with more credits a proportionally larger effect. Excluded grades are shown in the breakdown but do not enter the denominator.

Per course
Quality points = Grade points × Course credits
Overall GPA
GPA = Total quality points ÷ Total counted credits
Worked example: A 3-credit A (4.00), 4-credit B (3.00), and 2-credit A− (3.70) produce 31.40 quality points across 9 credits. GPA = 31.40 ÷ 9 = 3.49.

Tips and common mistakes

Most GPA differences come from the grading scale, course-credit weighting, inclusion rules, or rounding—not from the arithmetic itself.

Useful tips

  • Check the grading scale on the official transcript or institution website.
  • Enter course credits instead of treating every course equally.
  • Use the custom scale when the institution assigns different point values.
  • Check whether pass/fail, withdrawn, or incomplete grades are excluded.
  • Review high-credit courses because they have greater mathematical weight.
  • Keep the report for planning, but use the official transcript for formal submissions.

Common mistakes

  • Entering percentages as if they were grade points.
  • Giving every course the same credit value.
  • Including excluded grades incorrectly.
  • Assuming an A+ always equals 4.00.
  • Mixing a term GPA calculation with previous cumulative results.
  • Using a weighted high-school scale in an unweighted calculation.
  • Rounding individual course values too early.
  • Treating an estimate as an official institutional GPA.
