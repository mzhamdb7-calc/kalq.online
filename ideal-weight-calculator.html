Primary healthy range

Weight = BMI × height². The lower and upper healthy BMI values are converted into a height-specific weight range. This is the calculator’s primary result.

  • International: BMI 18.5–24.9
  • Malaysia / Asian: BMI 18.5–22.9
  • Compare mode displays both ranges together.

Formula set

Peterson uses height and a target BMI. Devine, Robinson, Miller and Hamwi apply historical height-based coefficients. Estimates below 152.4 cm are linear extrapolations.

  • Median reduces dependence on any single formula.
  • Spread shows how much the formulas disagree.
  • No body-fat percentage is inferred.
Why there is no pie chart: formula estimates are separate comparisons, not parts of a whole. A range scale and bar comparison are more accurate visualisations.

Use the result well

  • Treat the healthy range as a screening reference, not a compulsory target.
  • Compare current weight with the nearest range boundary rather than one exact formula value.
  • Use waist measurement and professional assessment when body composition matters.
  • Review trends over time instead of reacting to small daily changes.

When results need caution

  • Pregnancy, fluid retention, major illness or significant recent weight change.
  • Highly muscular builds, older adults and people with unusual body composition.
  • Medication dosing or clinical nutrition decisions.
  • Anyone under 18 should use age- and sex-specific growth references.
What is an ideal weight?

It is a height-based reference, not a perfect or required number. A range is more meaningful because formulas do not directly measure muscle, fat, bone structure or health conditions.

Which formula is most accurate?

No single formula is best for everyone. Use the healthy BMI range as the main result and compare the five estimates as supporting references.

Why do formulas give different answers?

They were developed from different datasets and use different starting values and height adjustments. The formula spread makes that uncertainty visible.

Why is the Malaysian range different?

Malaysian guidance uses a lower upper threshold for the normal range because health risks in Asian populations may occur at lower BMI levels.

Is BMI the same as body-fat percentage?

No. BMI compares weight with height and cannot distinguish fat, muscle, bone or fluid.

Can athletes use this calculator?

They can use it for general reference, but muscular users may receive BMI results that do not reflect body composition. Waist measurement or professional assessment adds context.

Can it be used during pregnancy or for children?

No. Pregnancy changes weight and fluid distribution, while people under 18 require age-specific growth charts.

Is the result a recommended weight-loss goal?

No. A safe goal depends on health status, current weight, body composition and professional advice.
