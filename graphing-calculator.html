What can this graphing calculator plot?

It plots mathematical functions, pasted x–y datasets and latest available daily currency reference-rate histories.

How should I enter a function?

Enter an expression such as x^2 − 4, sin(x), exp(x) or 1/x. You may also include y = or f(x) =.

Can I compare several functions?

Yes. Add up to four expressions. Each curve receives a separate color, legend entry and table column.

How are intercepts found?

The calculator scans the selected range for sign changes and near-zero values, then refines each crossing numerically.

Are extrema exact?

They are sampled numerical estimates. Increase sample density or narrow the graph window when higher visual precision is needed.

Can I paste spreadsheet data?

Yes. Paste two columns separated by commas, tabs, semicolons or spaces. A header row is ignored automatically.

Are the currency rates real-time trading prices?

No. They are the latest available daily reference rates from the selected feed. The observation date is shown clearly.

Can I export the graph and report?

Yes. Download the graph as PNG, the table as CSV, or save and print the complete A4 portrait report.

Why does a curve disappear?

The function may be undefined, outside the vertical window or changing too quickly. Reset the view or adjust the range and sample density.

Is the calculator suitable internationally?

Yes. Mathematics is universal, English is the default language, USD is the default currency for live-rate mode, and dates use the browser’s locale.

Data and input guidance

  • Use ^ for exponents, such as x^3.
  • Supported functions include sin, cos, tan, sqrt, abs, log, ln and exp.
  • Live FX data comes from the Frankfurter reference-rate API when internet access is available.
  • Graph and analysis results are numerical approximations.
