What an ideal-weight estimate means

No equation can identify one perfect weight for every person. This calculator presents several established estimates together so their differences remain visible.

Classic formula range

The lowest and highest values from Devine, Robinson, Miller and Hamwi form the classic estimate range.

Comparison midpoint

The median of the four results provides one central comparison figure without claiming a required target.

General healthy-weight reference

A broader height-based range using adult BMI values from 18.5 to 24.9. The calculator does not display a BMI score.

Current position

When current weight is added, the page shows its neutral position relative to both reference ranges.

Ideal-weight formulas

The traditional equations use height above or below 5 feet. The profile changes the starting value and height adjustment.

Devine
Male: 50 + 2.3 × inches from 5 ft · Female: 45.5 + 2.3 × inches from 5 ft
Robinson
Male: 52 + 1.9 × inches from 5 ft · Female: 49 + 1.7 × inches from 5 ft
Miller
Male: 55.7 + 1.41 × inches from 5 ft · Female: 53.1 + 1.36 × inches from 5 ft
Hamwi
Male: 106 lb + 6 lb per inch · Female: 100 lb + 5 lb per inch
Healthy reference: minimum weight = 18.5 × height²; maximum weight = 24.9 × height², with height measured in metres.

Tips and common mistakes

Accurate height entry and careful interpretation matter more than adding extra personal fields.

Useful tips

  • Measure height without shoes and stand upright.
  • Use the full range rather than focusing only on the midpoint.
  • Add current weight only when a comparison is useful.
  • Compare measurements under similar conditions.
  • Consider body composition, strength, energy and medical context.
  • Discuss major weight changes with a qualified professional.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the midpoint as a mandatory target.
  • Assuming one traditional formula must be correct.
  • Entering centimetres as metres or mixing unit systems.
  • Using decimal feet instead of feet and inches.
  • Using the adult calculator for children or teenagers.
  • Using the estimate for medication dosing or clinical decisions.

Calculation Guide

Formula notes, interpretation tips, common mistakes, FAQs, references and important limitations.

Formula method

Classic range = lowest to highest of four formulas
Midpoint = median of the four results
Healthy range = 18.5 × height² to 24.9 × height²

Tips

  • Measure height without shoes.
  • Use the full range, not only the midpoint.
  • Compare measurements consistently.
  • Consider body composition and health context.
  • Discuss major changes professionally.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the midpoint as mandatory
  • Mixing unit systems
  • Using decimal feet
  • Using adult formulas for children
  • Assuming weight equals body fat
  • Using the result for medication dosing

Quick FAQs

Is one formula best?No formula is universally accurate for every body type.
Why is current weight optional?Height alone is enough to create the formula estimates.
Should I reach the midpoint?Not automatically. It is a comparison value, not a prescription.
Does the result measure body fat?No. Height-and-weight methods do not measure body composition.
