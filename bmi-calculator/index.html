What your BMI result means

BMI compares weight with height. Adults use fixed BMI ranges, while children and teenagers use age- and sex-specific BMI-for-age percentiles.

Additional Asian adult health-risk guidance

Some Asian public-health guidance uses BMI 23.0 and 27.5 as additional adult risk action points. This applies to adult interpretation only and does not replace child and teen BMI-for-age percentiles.

BMI formula

The BMI equation is the same in both unit systems. The interpretation method changes according to age.

Metric
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²)
US units
BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height² (in²)
Worked example: For 70 kg and 1.75 m, BMI = 70 ÷ 1.75² = 22.9. Adults use the standard BMI ranges; ages 2–19 require BMI-for-age percentile interpretation.

Tips and common mistakes

Consistent measurements and correct unit entry matter more than adding extra fields. These checks improve calculation quality without complicating the calculator.

Measurement tips

  • Measure height without shoes and stand upright.
  • Use a recently calibrated scale where possible.
  • Compare results under similar measurement conditions.
  • Retake measurements when a result appears unexpected.
  • Review trends rather than reacting to one isolated result.
  • Interpret BMI together with other health information.

Common mistakes

  • Entering centimetres as metres.
  • Entering decimal feet instead of feet and inches.
  • Mixing pounds with the metric formula.
  • Rounding height before the calculation.
  • Applying adult categories to children or teenagers.
  • Treating BMI as a diagnosis or direct body-fat measurement.
  • Calling the calculated range an “ideal weight.”
