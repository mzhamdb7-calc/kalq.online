Mental Math Performance Calculator

Every result is calculated automatically from the round. Accuracy has the greatest weight, so uncontrolled guessing cannot outperform careful, fluent calculation.

Accuracy
Accuracy = Correct answers ÷ Questions attempted × 100
Correct per minute
CPM = Correct answers ÷ Playing seconds × 60
Average response
Average time = Total response time ÷ Questions attempted
Question points
Points = 100 × Difficulty × Operation × Speed × Streak
Consistency
100 − min(100, Response-time deviation ÷ Average time × 100)
Proficiency index
0.45 Accuracy + 0.25 Speed + 0.20 Difficulty + 0.10 Consistency

Transparent limitation: The Mental Math Proficiency Index is a KalQ game-performance indicator. It is not a standardized academic score, intelligence test, psychological assessment, or professional qualification.

What the professional report includes

The separate A4 portrait report uses the same typography and tri-colour system, but relies on open sections, tables, dividers, gauges, paths, and charts rather than repeated boxes.

Current performance analysis

Overall score, accuracy, correct answers per minute, average response time, consistency, highest level, best streak, and an easy-to-read written interpretation.

Skill and error analysis

Operation-by-operation accuracy and pace, difficulty reached, response-time trend, speed–accuracy position, fact heatmap, and classified mistakes such as sign, decimal, multiplication, division, percentage, timeout, and fast-guess errors.

Professional-use fields

Teacher or parent comments, practice target, review date, signature lines, verification code, print/PDF controls, and CSV export.

Future analysis

One session shows a baseline only. Two to four sessions show an early low-confidence direction. Five or more sessions show a seven-session trend, projected next rank, likely weak area, recommended difficulty, and confidence statement.

Seven-day practice plan

A personalised plan alternates weak-topic practice, untimed accuracy, mixed recall, saved-error review, speed rounds, adaptive challenge, and reassessment.

Certificate logic

Certificate requirements rise with difficulty. Beginner and Easy are intentionally accessible; Medium and Hard require stronger proof; Expert, Master, and Legend demand high accuracy and more validated questions. Thresholds are 70%, 74%, 80%, 86%, 91%, 95%, and 98%, with total-question requirements from 8 to 30. Harder questions may validate a lower certificate when the higher certificate threshold is missed. Bronze begins at 70%, Silver at 85%, Gold at 90%, and Diamond at 95%.

Tips and common mistakes

Useful mental strategies improve performance more than adding complicated setup fields. The report links repeated mistakes to the most relevant practice action.

Mental-math tips

  • Build accuracy before pushing maximum speed.
  • Break large numbers into tens, hundreds, or convenient factors.
  • Use complements to 10, 100, and 1,000.
  • Learn doubles, halves, and near-doubles.
  • Multiply by 25 by dividing by 4 and multiplying by 100.
  • Multiply by 5 by multiplying by 10 and halving.
  • Multiply by 9 by multiplying by 10 and subtracting the original number.
  • Use distribution, such as 48 × 7 = 40 × 7 + 8 × 7.
  • Convert 50%, 25%, and 10% into simple fractions.
  • Estimate the answer range before completing a hard calculation.
  • Review missed questions instead of repeating only strong topics.
  • Prefer short sessions across several days to one exhausting session.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing after a long streak and guessing to protect the combo.
  • Misreading the operation symbol during mixed rounds.
  • Losing a negative sign.
  • Moving a decimal point incorrectly.
  • Using a multiplication fact when division is required.
  • Practising only comfortable operations to protect a high score.
  • Comparing raw scores from different difficulty settings.
  • Treating one round as a permanent measure of ability.
  • Using speed as the only success measure.
  • Calling the proficiency index an IQ or intelligence score.

How to interpret this report

  • Use accuracy and error patterns before raw score.
  • Compare sessions with similar settings.
  • Look for trends across several days.
  • Use the practice plan as a starting point, not a fixed prescription.
  • Combine the report with teacher observation and broader work.

Important limitations

  • The index is specific to this game.
  • One session cannot establish stable ability.
  • Device, interruptions, familiarity, and question mix affect performance.
  • Forecasts are estimates and never guarantees.
  • Certificates record game achievement, not formal qualification.
