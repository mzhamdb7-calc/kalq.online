Build a stronger projection

  • Base retirement spending on an expense budget, not only a salary percentage.
  • Review results in today’s purchasing power and future nominal values.
  • Include employer contributions, investment fees and reliable pension income.
  • Test lower returns and a longer retirement to understand downside risk.

Practical next steps

  • Increase contributions gradually when income rises.
  • Review the plan at least once a year and after major life changes.
  • Compare retiring later with reducing planned retirement spending.
  • Confirm pension, tax and withdrawal rules with official local sources.
How much money do I need to retire?

The target depends on planned spending, pension income, retirement duration, inflation, returns, fees and taxes. This calculator estimates the amount required from those assumptions.

Are the rates live?

Currency conversion uses the latest available working-day reference rate. Inflation loads the latest non-null World Bank observation for the selected country. Investment returns remain editable planning assumptions rather than guaranteed live returns.

Why are today’s values useful?

Today’s values remove estimated inflation, making future retirement income easier to compare with current living costs.

What return should I enter?

Use a cautious long-term assumption after fees. Test at least one lower-return scenario because recent market performance may not continue.

How is the retirement target calculated?

The calculator estimates the starting fund needed to cover inflation-adjusted spending after pension income until the planning end age.

Can I use this outside the United States?

Yes. Select a country and currency, then edit retirement age, pension income, tax and investment assumptions to suit local rules.

Does this include government pensions?

Enter an estimated monthly pension or other reliable retirement income. Use an official pension statement where available.

What happens if I retire earlier?

You have less time to save and more years to fund, so the required contribution or retirement target normally increases.

How are taxes handled?

The optional effective tax rate increases the gross portfolio withdrawal needed to fund planned net spending. It is a simplified estimate.

Is the result guaranteed?

No. It is an educational projection. Actual returns, inflation, fees, tax rules, lifespan and spending will differ.
