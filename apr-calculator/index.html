What your result meansThis page is deliberately specific: it estimates APR for a standard fixed-rate, equal-monthly-payment loan rather than mixing APR with affordability, credit score or approval tools.

What APR tells you

APR is an annualized measure of credit cost. On a simple closed-end installment loan, applicable upfront fees can make APR higher than the stated interest rate because the borrower receives less net credit while making the scheduled payments.

What the monthly payment tells you

The monthly payment is calculated from the full loan amount, stated annual interest rate and number of monthly payments. It shows cash-flow impact, while APR focuses on annualized credit cost.

What total borrowing cost tells you

Total borrowing cost in this calculator equals scheduled interest plus the entered upfront fees. It complements APR by translating borrowing cost into dollars.

Scope and assumption

The calculator assumes one initial loan advance, equal monthly payments, a fixed stated rate, and entered fees deducted from proceeds rather than added to principal. Irregular payment dates, financed fees, variable rates and open-end credit need different treatment.

FormulaThe payment is calculated from the stated loan rate. APR is then solved from the amount financed and payment stream using an iterative present-value equation.
Payment
Payment = P × r(1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)
Amount financed
Amount Financed = Loan Amount − Entered Upfront Fees
Periodic APR rate
Amount Financed = Σ Payment ÷ (1 + i)t, for t = 1 … n
APR
APR = i × 12 × 100

Tips and common mistakesUse APR as one comparison measure, then check the payment, term and total dollar cost. Keep fee treatment consistent when comparing offers.

Tips

  • Compare APRs, not only advertised rates. Applicable fees can change the annualized cost.
  • Check which upfront fees apply. Origination and other charges can affect the amount financed and APR treatment.
  • Compare similar loan terms. A different term changes payment size and total dollars paid.
  • Look at APR and total cost together. Percentage and dollar views answer different questions.
  • Review official disclosures. Use the lender's final documents for the transaction-specific APR.
  • Recalculate when fees change. Even with the same rate and term, fees can move APR.

Common mistakes

  • Entering APR as the interest rate. Use the stated annual interest rate in the rate field.
  • Leaving out relevant upfront fees. Omitting them can understate the estimated APR.
  • Assuming APR equals interest rate. The two measures can differ when applicable charges are present.
  • Comparing APRs across very different terms. Term, payment and total cost still matter.
  • Treating the estimate as an official disclosure. Actual lender APR can depend on transaction details and rules.
  • Assuming every fee belongs in APR. Fee treatment can vary; enter only fees appropriate for the comparison you are making.
